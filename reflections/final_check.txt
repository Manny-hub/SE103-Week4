What I understand better about algorithms now:
An algorithm is a precise list of ordered steps, not a loose idea. Good pseudocode names its inputs, its outputs, and the edge cases it must handle. Loops and decisions are the main building blocks for problems like counting and searching. Writing the empty-list check first prevents errors later in the process. I also learned that vague wording is the most common source of confusion.

How tracing changed my confidence:
Tracing let me test my pseudocode by hand with real values. Seeing the count reach 2 for the sample input showed me the logic worked. It also helped me spot missing steps before writing any code.

One pseudocode habit I will keep:
I will always handle the empty or no-result case explicitly. It is easy to forget, and it makes the algorithm much more reliable.
